# Product Validation Playbook

## Goal
Select products using observable customer problems and purchase intent, not enthusiasm or search volume alone.

## Evidence log
For every signal, record the date, URL, platform/language, customer problem, urgency/workaround, willingness-to-pay evidence, sample limitations, access method, allowed use, and confidence level. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.

## Validation sequence
1. Problem discovery: find repeated, specific pain points from independent sources.
2. Alternatives: compare current solutions and their shortcomings.
3. Purchase intent: look for explicit requests, paid alternatives, buyer questions, or verifiable transactions.
4. Prototype: build the smallest useful deliverable.
5. User test: observe whether target users can complete the promised job.
6. Offer test: use a compliant channel and honest listing; never fabricate orders/reviews.
7. Decide: continue, revise, or stop based on documented criteria.

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each)
Problem frequency; pain/urgency; willingness-to-pay evidence; reachability without spam; differentiation; build speed; low support burden; rights/policy safety.

Subtract risk for high competition, uncertain platform eligibility, high support needs, or paid infrastructure dependence. Scores guide discussion; they do not prove demand.

## Stop conditions
Pause if evidence is weak, the deliverable is unusable, rights are unclear, the channel disallows the format, or unit economics are negative after fees and support time.

## Evidence integrity
Never invent sales, citations, testimonials, market size, or interviews. Label hypotheses and snapshots with date and source.
